## 0. How to use this document

This is the fifth canonical PacketPulse 2.0 reference document. It extends, rather than replaces, the existing Design System and UX Guide.

- Document 1 defines product vision, requirements and decisions.
- Document 2 defines the brand, design tokens, accessibility, layout and UX rules.
- Document 3 defines users, screens and page flows.
- Document 4 defines delivery tasks, developer handoff and acceptance tests.
- **Document 5 defines the component ecosystem, reusable UI patterns, source-library registry and the detailed implementation requirements for the owner-supplied visual references REF-00 through REF-06 and later PacketPulse design concepts.**

When two documents appear to conflict, use the newer owner-approved decision recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`. An owner-approved prototype may be built and reviewed in the next review build. ## 0.1 Owner direction: Be open, creative and experimental

The product owner explicitly directs the team to **be open, creative and experimental**. Treat this as a design and product-development principle, not as a request to abandon consistency or quality.

- Do not reject a visual idea, interaction, component, library, or capability solely because it is unfamiliar, unconventional, or outside the current component set.
- Explore the idea in the context of a real PacketPulse user need. Ask what it improves: clarity, confidence, belonging, delight, task completion, discovery, credibility, or development speed.
- When the right approach is uncertain, create a lightweight prototype or side-by-side variants instead of debating in the abstract. A useful default is to explore two or three materially different treatments for high-impact or uncertain experiences.
- Allow controlled visual experiments beyond the current palette or layout conventions in the exploration stage. Before promotion into a shared production component, map the winning treatment to Signal Grove semantic tokens and document any deliberate exception.
- Use feature flags, limited cohorts, reversible configuration, or isolated routes for behavioural experiments where appropriate. Keep experiments observable and reversible.
- Define a team review window for significant experiments. Collect member feedback, usability findings, accessibility results, performance, error rates and relevant product outcomes. At review, retain, refine, restrict, pause or remove the experiment based on evidence.
- Do not mistake novelty for value. Experimental work must still respect privacy, security, accessibility, performance, licensing and operational constraints.
- Avoid prematurely freezing the design system. It should provide a coherent foundation while leaving room for new patterns and better ideas.

### Experiment maturity states

| State | Meaning | Expected evidence before promotion |
|---|---|---|
| Spark | An idea worth exploring; no implementation commitment | User/problem hypothesis and inspiration/source note |
| Prototype | A quick, reversible demonstration | Visual/interaction comparison and feasibility notes |
| Pilot | Limited real use, cohort or feature-flag rollout | Defined review window, owner, success measures and safeguards |
| Approved | Team/product review supports continued use | Feedback summary, accessibility and technical review |
| Standard | Adopted into the shared PacketPulse system | Documentation, component API, tests, maintenance owner |
| Retired | Removed or replaced after review | Reason, migration or replacement note where applicable |

An experiment can be valuable even when it is not promoted. Record what was learned so the team does not repeat the same exploration without new evidence.

## 0.2 Experience principle: Make the product feel good to use

Whitespace, visual rhythm and emotional tone are product requirements. The interface should feel calm, capable, welcoming, credible and quietly rewarding. Do not fill available space with widgets merely because the viewport can hold them. Prefer a clear visual path, a meaningful focal point, and enough breathing room for users to understand what matters.

Use microanimations where they acknowledge an action, explain a state change, establish spatial relationships or make progress understandable. Do not add motion only for decoration. Preserve reduced-motion behaviour and keep all information understandable without animation.

## 1.2 What must not be copied literally

- Do not reuse the reference's sample names, people, dates, company data, percentages or metrics as PacketPulse data.
- Treat reference colours as inspiration, not restrictions. Explore the full Signal Grove palette, module accents, experimental hues, tonal ramps, multi-colour gradients and Pantone-referenced studies; record and test the chosen digital values.
- Treat 3D/perspective carousels as an expressive option for curated feature/collection discovery where they add value; also prototype calmer or simpler carousel variants and compare. - Do not use the numbered feature cards as the default treatment for all dashboard cards; explore them where storytelling, onboarding or structured navigation benefits.
- Let whitespace and hierarchy guide density; don't compress a screen simply to fit more widgets into the first viewport.
- Do not treat visual similarity as proof of accessibility, responsive behaviour, code quality or licence permission.

# 2. Signal Grove visual language for these patterns

## 2.1 One design system, multiple workspace moods

PacketPulse uses one Signal Grove token system with two layout families and deliberate theme variants:

1. **Editorial Workspace:** Community discussions, technical articles, learning content, profiles, mentoring and discovery. It prioritises reading comfort, content hierarchy, human identity and whitespace.
2. **Analytical Workspace:** Operations, recruitment analytics, community analytics, administrative queues and performance workspaces. It prioritises scanning, comparison, data clarity and drill-down.

The Analytical Workspace has two intentional appearances:

- **Matte Black Edition:** Preferred reference for data-heavy administrative and analytical workspaces, based on the owner's positive response to the matte-black dashboard reference.
- **Soft Signal Light:** Light counterpart inspired by REF-01. Particularly suitable for people/participation summaries, scheduling, community overviews and users who prefer light mode.

Both themes use the same component API, semantic tokens, information hierarchy and interaction rules. Theme choice must not change the meaning of data or the available actions. Respect the member's saved preference when a screen supports personal theme selection; organisation policy may set a default but should not silently remove the user's choice unless a separate product requirement approves that behaviour.

## 2.3 Module accents

Use the existing module identities from Document 2:

- Community / Brand: Forest.
- Learning: Indigo.
- Recruitment: Blue.
- Payments: Plum.
- Operations: neutral/brand shell, with semantic state colours.

A component may accept a `tone` or `module` variant, but it must use semantic tokens rather than raw page-level hex values. Success, warning, danger and information always retain their semantic meaning. A module accent must never impersonate a success or verification state.

## 2.4 Whitespace and density

Whitespace is a deliberate UX tool. It establishes grouping, lowers perceived effort, improves scan paths and creates a calm product atmosphere. It is not unused space to be filled with widgets.

| Context | Default spacing guidance |
|---|---|
| Page edge | 24–40px desktop; 16–20px mobile |
| H1 to description | 8–12px |
| Description to first section | 24–32px |
| Major section separation | 40–64px desktop; 28–40px mobile |
| Standard card padding | 20–24px desktop; 16px mobile |
| Small status card padding | 12–16px |
| Form field groups | 20–24px |
| Reading line length | Aim for 60–75 characters |
| Dashboard grid gap | 16–24px, selected by density and viewport |

Density modes:

- **Comfortable:** Default for member dashboard, feed, cards, people panels and forms.
- **Compact:** Use for high-volume tables, review queues, candidate comparison and admin lists. Keep text readable and touch targets accessible.
- **Reading:** Use for article, question/answer, documentation and technical content.

Do not create empty space by hiding useful information; create it by grouping, prioritising and progressively disclosing secondary detail.

## 2.5 Shape, border and elevation

- Standard cards: 14–18px radius; larger feature panels may use 20–24px.
- File upload cards: 16–20px radius; compact rows may use 12–14px.
- Feature tiles: 20–24px radius, with clear internal padding.
- Inputs and buttons: follow the existing 10px radius token; pill shapes are reserved for chips and selected compact controls.
- Prefer a subtle 1px border and tonal surface separation over heavy shadows.
- Shadows are reserved for actual floating layers (menus, popovers, dialogs), not every card.
- Hover must not cause surrounding layout shift.

# 3. Reference pattern specifications

## 3.1 PP-DASH-001 — Spacious People & Community Analytics Dashboard

**Source inspiration:** REF-01.  
**Workspace:** Analytical Workspace, Soft Signal Light variant; compatible with Matte Black tokens.  
**Primary use:** Community overview, member participation, people/mentor operations, learning engagement and other dashboards that mix metrics with schedules or people.

### Layout anatomy

1. **Top utility bar**
   - Global search on the left with a visible keyboard hint (e.g. `⌘ K` / `Ctrl K`).
   - Optional AI/help assistant entry point, if the feature is enabled for that role.
   - Notifications with unread count.
   - Member avatar and account menu on the right.
   - Utility controls must remain compact and must not compete with the page title.

2. **Page header**
   - Breadcrumb where depth requires it.
   - One clear H1 and concise description.
   - Date range, workspace selector or primary action aligned to the right.
   - On mobile, selectors may move into a filter sheet.

3. **KPI strip**
   - Usually 3–5 cards. Four is a good starting arrangement for REF-01-style dashboards.
   - One featured metric may use a deeper forest surface with a high-contrast number and a small sparkline.
   - Remaining KPI cards use neutral surfaces, clear label/value/period comparison and a restrained mini-visual where useful.
   - Each metric must define its period, unit and comparison baseline. A number without a definition is not an approved KPI.
   - Avoid decorative mini charts if the data cannot be interpreted or if they duplicate the main chart.

4. **Primary analysis area**
   - Use a clear 8/4 or 7/5 grid depending on the right rail content.
   - Main panel may include chart, distribution, percentage breakdown or activity analysis.
   - Show a title, metric unit, period selector, legend and direct labels or tooltip details.
   - A tooltip can reveal detail but must not contain the only explanation of a metric.
   - Pattern fills/hatching may support series differentiation, but colour alone must never be the only distinction.

5. **Contextual calendar / schedule panel**
   - Month and date navigation.
   - Selected day, today, event day and disabled date must be visually distinct and keyboard operable.
   - Below the calendar, show a concise agenda list for the selected day.
   - Mobile: calendar collapses to a compact date strip or opens in a sheet; agenda remains a readable list.

6. **Supporting insight panels**
   - Use a two-column arrangement for short explanation, happiness/health signal, next action, or overview text.
   - One panel may have a slightly stronger background or accent; do not give every panel a different colour.
   - Each panel has a clear title, short explanatory copy and at most one obvious action.

7. **People / contributor panels**
   - Member avatar, display name, area/skill, relevant tags, participation context and one appropriate action.
   - Avatar groups can show a small set of additional participants, with a `+N` overflow label.
   - Do not show follower counts or vanity scores as the primary credibility signal.
   - Keep privacy and profile visibility settings intact.

### Visual hierarchy

- KPI value is the strongest element inside a metric card; metric label and comparison follow.
- Use tabular numerals for counts, dates and percentages.
- One featured KPI is enough; all cards should not compete for hero treatment.
- Chart occupies the largest visual area because it answers the dashboard's primary question.
- Calendar and people panels are secondary context, not equal-weight competitors.
- Use a restrained green/lime accent; lime is for selected days, key milestone or one meaningful emphasis, not all chart bars.

### Responsive behaviour

| Viewport | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| ≥1280px | Full KPI strip; main analysis and contextual rail side-by-side; supporting panels below |
| 1024–1279px | KPI grid may wrap; right rail may narrow or collapse |
| 768–1023px | Two-column KPI cards; calendar and people context move below chart or into collapsible panel |
| <768px | Single-column flow; no horizontal page overflow; compact utility bar; calendar uses sheet/date strip |
| <640px | KPI cards may use 2 columns only if labels and values remain clear; otherwise stacked list |

### States and interactions

- Loading: skeletons preserve the final layout dimensions; avoid flashing empty cards.
- Empty: explain why there is no data and provide a next action (e.g. invite members, select period, create event).
- Error: show what failed, keep selected filters, provide retry.
- Metric drill-down: clicking a KPI should navigate or open a meaningful detail view; do not make numbers look clickable if they are not.
- Chart hover/focus: keyboard-accessible inspection and text-equivalent summaries.
- Calendar date selection: visibly update agenda panel and announce selection accessibly.

### Build notes

Use the shared `AnalyticalWorkspaceShell`, `MetricCard`, `ChartPanel`, `CalendarPanel`, `PeopleListCard` and `InsightPanel`. The page must not be implemented as one monolithic dashboard component.

## 3.2 PP-UPLOAD-001 — File Transfer / Upload Card

**Source inspiration:** REF-02, REF-03 and REF-04.  
**Use:** Community technical questions, learning submissions, resumes, certifications, verification evidence, profile media and administrative attachments.

The three images represent related states of one component, not three unrelated designs.

### Component anatomy

- File-type icon or thumbnail.
- Filename (truncate only after a useful visible portion; full name available to screen readers and detail tooltip).
- File size and optional file type.
- Status label and, when uploading, progress percentage and transferred/total bytes.
- Progress rail with accessible text value.
- State-dependent controls: pause, resume, cancel, retry, remove, change file and download.
- Optional security/validation status (e.g. scanning, file type rejected, sensitive-data warning).

### Required state model

| State | Visual treatment | Available action |
|---|---|---|
| Idle / empty | Drop zone or browse button; accepted formats and max size stated | Choose file / drag and drop |
| Selected / queued | File icon, name, size and queued label | Start, remove |
| Uploading | Progress rail, percentage, transferred/total size | Pause, cancel |
| Paused | Frozen progress, explicit Paused label | Resume, cancel |
| Processing / scanning | Indeterminate progress or staged status; do not fake percentage | View status, cancel only if supported |
| Completed | Success glyph, Completed text, file size | Change, download, remove if permitted |
| Failed | Error glyph, concise reason, retained filename | Retry, change, remove |
| Canceled | Canceled label, no success semantics | Retry or remove |
| Rejected | Policy reason (format, size, malware or policy) | Change file, view guidance |

### Reference states

- **REF-02 Uploading:** Place filename and file identity on the first line. Show percentage and bytes transferred on the second line. Progress rail spans most of the card width. Pause and cancel remain clearly separate controls.
- **REF-03 Completed compact:** Use a single-row version for completed lists. Show file icon, success glyph, status and file size; removal action is visually separated and labelled accessibly.
- **REF-04 Completed actions:** Use an expanded footer with secondary `Change` and primary `Download` buttons when the next task requires the file. If download is not relevant (e.g. uploaded evidence), replace it with the appropriate approved next action.

### Progress behaviour

- Progress value must come from actual transfer events, not a decorative timer.
- Show a numerical percentage when reliable. If the upload is indeterminate, use an indeterminate progress state and explanatory label.
- Pause/resume is shown only if supported by the upload transport/provider. Do not show a fake pause button.
- Cancel is not the same as delete. Cancel stops an active transfer; remove deletes a queued/completed item from the current form according to the product's storage rules.
- Completion state must be stable; do not auto-dismiss a file needed for the next step.
- If a file is replaced, clarify whether the old file is removed immediately or after the replacement succeeds.

### Validation and safety

- Display accepted formats and maximum size before upload.
- Validate size and extension client-side for fast feedback and server-side for enforcement.
- Verify file content/type server-side; extension alone is not trusted.
- Scan files and quarantine unsafe attachments before making them accessible.
- For technical uploads, add sensitive-data warning/redaction guidance where applicable.
- Never expose an attachment to other users until the server-side access policy permits it.
- For long/resumable uploads, select a transport that actually supports pause/resume and recovery. Evaluate the existing backend first; Uppy or a provider-native resumable approach is a candidate, not a default dependency.

### Accessibility

- Progress uses `role="progressbar"` or the appropriate native element with accessible name, `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax` where determinate.
- Announce state changes (uploading, paused, completed, failed) without announcing every percent tick.
- Buttons have explicit names (`Pause upload`, `Cancel upload`, `Remove file`, `Download file`).
- Do not rely on green/red alone; show icon and text.
- Preserve keyboard navigation and visible focus.

### Responsive behaviour

On mobile, stack metadata and actions naturally. Keep primary status visible; place secondary actions in an overflow menu only when necessary. Do not make the progress rail too thin to perceive or tap its controls too small.

### Component API direction (illustrative, not final code)

```tsx
<FileTransfer
  file={file}
  status="uploading"
  progress={65}
  transferredBytes={1_400_000}
  totalBytes={2_300_000}
  onPause={pauseUpload}
  onCancel={cancelUpload}
  onRetry={retryUpload}
  onRemove={removeFile}
  onDownload={downloadFile}
  onReplace={replaceFile}
  density="comfortable"
/>
```

The API must not expose unsupported actions. Use controlled state from the upload service; do not have the visual component invent or simulate upload completion.

## 3.3 PP-CAROUSEL-001 — Perspective Featured Card Carousel

**Source inspiration:** REF-05.  
**Use:** Featured mentors, expert contributors, upcoming events, curated learning resources, community projects and selected case studies.

### Visual style

- Horizontal track with one clearly active centre card and adjacent cards partially visible.
- Active card may be slightly larger or sharper; adjacent cards may be scaled down or subtly angled.
- Use restrained perspective (`perspective` / `rotateY`) as a progressive enhancement, never as a requirement for understanding content.
- Active content includes image/portrait, title/name, category or role, and a small set of useful metadata.
- A separate metadata strip may show relevant facts (e.g. availability, skill area, reading time, event date), not arbitrary vanity values.
- Keep surrounding canvas calm; no glowing neon rails or continuous auto-motion.

### Interaction

- Support drag/swipe, previous/next controls and keyboard navigation.
- Make active index visible via dots, count or selected card semantics where appropriate.
- Do not auto-advance by default. If auto-advance is introduced, provide pause/stop controls and a respectful interval; avoid auto-advancing content that users are reading.
- Clicking a card opens its detail page or performs a clearly indicated action.
- Ensure partially visible cards do not trap focus or hide essential controls.

### Responsive behaviour

- Desktop: show active centre card plus partial neighbours.
- Tablet: reduce side peek and perspective; allow drag/swipe.
- Mobile: use a single-card swipe layout with visible next-card hint; keep navigation buttons accessible.
- Reduced motion: remove 3D rotation and animated scale; use a simple horizontal scroll/instant active state.

### Appropriate PacketPulse uses

- Featured mentors/expert contributors.
- Upcoming AMAs or technical events.
- Curated courses, labs or learning paths.
- Member project/case-study showcases.
- Featured community spaces.

### Do not use for

- Primary question feed.
- Long lists, tables or search results.
- Important alerts or transactional controls.
- Interfaces where users must compare many items precisely.

## 3.4 PP-FEATURE-TILE-001 — Numbered Editorial Feature Cards

**Source inspiration:** REF-06.  
**Use:** Product marketing, community onboarding, learning pathways, step-by-step setup, value proposition sections and curated feature explanation.

### Visual anatomy

1. A tall portrait-oriented card with a large number or sequence marker (`01`, `02`, `03`).
2. A visual zone in the upper/central area: illustration, abstract pattern, product screenshot crop or restrained gradient.
3. A clear heading and short description near the lower section.
4. A small directional affordance (arrow) showing that the card can be opened or explored.
5. A layered panel or inset surface that gives the card visual depth without excessive shadow.

### Style rules

- Use a coherent family of surfaces: one forest/teal card, one neutral/light card, one dark/raised card where the story benefits from variation.
- Numbering must be consistent in position, size and visual meaning.
- Keep the title concise; description should normally be 1–3 short lines.
- A card should have one clear destination/action.
- Use a single shared card layout, not three unrelated designs.
- The card's visual zone must not be a decorative placeholder without meaning. Prefer custom illustrations or real product imagery.
- In Matte Black mode, use tonal surface separation and a restrained accent; do not simply invert the reference's bright gradient.

### Interaction

- Whole card is clickable only if it leads to a single destination; otherwise keep a clear button/link.
- Hover may raise the card by 1–2px, move the arrow by 2–4px or reveal a subtle border/gradient change.
- Focus state must be as visible as hover.
- No auto-playing video or continuous gradient animation.
- Reduced-motion users receive a static card with the same hierarchy.

### Appropriate PacketPulse uses

- `Learn → Practice → Prove` pathway.
- Community onboarding: discover, ask, contribute.
- Product capability storytelling.
- How verification works.
- Getting-started guides for organisations.

### Do not use for

- Repeated dashboard KPI cards.
- Dense admin lists.
- Transaction status or verification decision details.
- Long explanatory text.

# 5. Component ecosystem and library registry

## 5.1 Adoption architecture

PacketPulse must own the final component API and visual behaviour. External libraries are sources and foundations, not competing product design systems.

Recommended layers:

1. **Foundation:** Tailwind CSS v4, CSS variables, semantic tokens, typography, spacing and motion tokens.
2. **Accessible primitives:** Use the primitive family already present in the repository. New shadcn projects currently default to Base UI; Radix remains supported. Do not mix both arbitrarily or migrate a stable app without a clear technical reason.
3. **Source-owned components:** shadcn/ui is the preferred base where appropriate; components are copied into the repo and adapted.
4. **Curated registries:** Ingot UI, BoardUI, Beautiful UI and Bencho may supply specific components after a code, licence, accessibility and theme audit.
5. **PacketPulse wrappers:** `PPButton`, `PPDialog`, `PPDataTable`, `PPFileTransfer`, etc. expose stable product-specific APIs and Signal Grove tokens.
6. **Product compositions:** Analytical Workspace, Community Composer, Verification Review Panel and other screen-level assemblies.

External code should be imported into a controlled location, reviewed, and then adapted behind the PacketPulse component API. Do not allow a library's default design tokens, font, colours or icon style to become global product defaults.

# 6. Motion and microinteraction patterns

Motion must make state changes understandable, acknowledge input or clarify spatial relationships. It must not be an attention trap or make a task slower.

## 6.1 Runtime and workflow

- Preferred runtime candidate: Motion for React, subject to repository/dependency audit.
- Use CSS transitions for simple hover, focus and colour changes; do not add JavaScript animation for a simple CSS job.
- Transitions.dev may provide recipes, audit guidance or an AI-agent skill/MCP workflow. It is not automatically a second runtime library.
- Bencho and UI Feed may inform interaction ideas. Copying an effect requires source/licence review and adaptation to the PacketPulse motion tokens.

## 6.2 Motion tokens

| Token | Starting duration | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| `--motion-instant` | 100–120ms | Immediate pressed/selected feedback |
| `--motion-quick` | 120–180ms | Button, bookmark, toggle, check and icon feedback |
| `--motion-standard` | 160–240ms | Tabs, tooltip, toast, dropdown and small overlays |
| `--motion-emphasis` | 240–350ms | Drawers, expandable panels and card layout changes |
| `--motion-milestone` | 350–700ms | A meaningful achievement, assessment result or verification milestone |

Durations are starting values, not an excuse to animate everything. Prefer opacity and transform. Avoid `transition: all`; animate named properties. Do not animate expensive filters, large blur layers or continuously repainting backgrounds.

## 6.3 Approved interaction candidates

| Pattern | PacketPulse use | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Number subtle change / pop-in | KPI updates | Use for changed values, not every page load; keep data legible |
| Progress rail fill | File transfer, assessments and onboarding | Reflect real progress; do not fake completion |
| Spinner-to-check / success check | Save or verification completion | Use only after server confirms success |
| Skeleton-to-content crossfade | Dashboard/feed loading | Keep layout stable; no shimmer under reduced motion |
| Panel reveal | Contextual rail, drawer and details | Preserve focus and reading order |
| Sliding tabs | Feed views and analytics periods | Active state must remain understandable without motion |
| Avatar group hover | Participant preview | Desktop enhancement; mobile gets tap/click equivalent |
| Card resize | Expandable insight and dashboard panels | Do not reflow unrelated content abruptly |
| Toast open/close | Low-impact operation feedback | High-impact outcomes must persist on the page or in history |
| Error state | Form or upload failure | Use clear inline explanation; avoid aggressive shake |
| Feature tile hover | Editorial feature cards | Small lift/arrow shift; not 3D on every tile |

## 6.4 Reduced motion and accessible interaction

- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` throughout the app.
- Under reduced motion, remove 3D rotation, parallax, shimmer, animated number drums, large scale transitions and decorative entry effects.
- Retain state changes, completion icons, progress values and status text.
- Never communicate state solely through animation.
- Do not auto-advance the featured carousel by default.
- Do not cause vestibular discomfort through pointer-driven 3D tilt or fast perspective movement.

# 7. Responsive and accessibility requirements

## 7.1 Responsive layout

- Use existing breakpoints from Document 2: 640, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1536px.
- Analytical Workspace: full sidebar and contextual rail at wide desktop; collapse the rail on smaller desktop/tablet; one-column layout on mobile.
- KPI strips wrap predictably; do not make users horizontally scroll to discover the first key metrics.
- Tables may become stacked records on small screens; keep essential fields and actions visible.
- File upload actions may wrap/stack; filename and status must remain visible.
- Carousels become swipeable horizontal tracks with a clear active item; no compulsory perspective effect.
- Numbered feature cards stack vertically on small screens; maintain sequence ordering.

## 7.2 Accessibility release checks

- WCAG 2.2 AA remains the release target.
- Keyboard access for every interactive control and composite widget.
- Visible focus indicator and logical focus order.
- Text labels accompany semantic colours and icons.
- Chart data has a text summary and table/list alternative.
- File progress and state updates are announced accessibly.
- Carousel supports keyboard, touch and reduced motion; avoid unexpected focus movement.
- Feature cards use semantic headings and links/buttons, not clickable generic containers without keyboard semantics.
- Calendar uses an accessible grid or equivalent date selection pattern and announces selected date.
- Test 200% zoom, narrow viewport, high contrast and screen-reader navigation.

# 8. Implementation guidance for Claude and developers

## 8.1 Repository-first rule

Before implementation, record:

- Next.js and React versions.
- Tailwind version and integration mode.
- Current shadcn `components.json` style and primitive family.
- Existing Radix/Base UI packages and component usage.
- Current chart, table, editor, upload, icon and motion dependencies.
- Existing component folders and aliases.
- Current light/dark theme implementation.
- Build, lint, typecheck, test and storybook/catalogue commands.

Do not install all candidate libraries. Choose one source for each required behaviour after this audit. Preserve existing working dependencies unless replacement is justified by a documented need.

## 8.2 Tailwind rule

Tailwind CSS v4 is the styling standard.

- Tailwind Play CDN may be used for throwaway prototypes, isolated demos or exploration.
- Do not use the browser Play CDN in production Next.js.
- Production uses compiled Tailwind CSS v4 through the app's build integration.
- Keep theme values in CSS variables / semantic theme tokens and map them to Tailwind utilities.
- Do not place raw hex values in page components or individual copied library components.

## 8.3 Suggested package decision matrix

| Need | First choice | Alternative / candidate | Install rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared primitives | Existing shadcn setup | Base UI or Radix, according to repo | No primitive migration without audit |
| Dashboard ready-made blocks | BoardUI or Ingot UI | Beautiful UI | Use small selected components; verify licence and dark theme |
| Specialised component | shadcn registry | Bencho / Beautiful UI | Copy and review source into repo |
| Table behaviour | TanStack Table | Existing table package | Adopt only for advanced behaviour |
| Chart rendering | Existing chart package | Recharts | Use one chart renderer, not several |
| Animation | Motion for React | CSS transitions | One runtime motion engine; CSS for simple states |
| Motion recipes | Transitions.dev | Bencho examples | Guidance/source only; no duplicate runtime |
| Rich editor | Existing editor | Tiptap | Audit schema, security, collaboration and accessibility |
| Simple drag/drop | Native input / react-dropzone | Uppy | Uppy only for resumable/complex upload needs |
| Icons | Lucide | Simple Icons for vendor marks | Keep UI icon family consistent |
| Toasts | Existing toast system | Sonner | Avoid duplicate toast providers |

## 8.4 Component acceptance definition

A component is **Approved** only when:

1. It serves a named PacketPulse use case.
2. The source, licence and attribution conditions are recorded.
3. Dependencies are justified and compatible with the repository.
4. It uses Signal Grove semantic tokens in both supported themes.
5. Comfortable/Compact/Reading density is defined where applicable.
6. Loading, empty, success, disabled and error states are documented where relevant.
7. Keyboard, focus and screen-reader behaviour are tested.
8. Mobile/tablet behaviour is reviewed.
9. Reduced-motion behaviour is tested for animated components.
10. Visual regression and functional tests cover important states.
11. It has a stable PP component ID and catalogue page.
12. The owning module and maintainer are identified.

## 8.5 Suggested component folder structure

Adapt this to the existing repository; do not restructure it blindly.

```text
src/
  components/
    ui/                    # primitive controls and wrappers
    shared/                # cross-product PacketPulse components
    workspace/             # analytical/editorial layout compositions
    community/
    learning/
    recruitment/
    payments/
    operations/
  styles/
    tokens.css
    themes.css
    motion.css
  lib/
    component-utils/
    asset-provenance/
```

A component sourced from another registry should be reviewed, adapted and placed under a PacketPulse-owned name and API. Keep a source reference in the registry; do not leave product pages coupled directly to a third-party registry import if it would make future replacement difficult.

**Desktop composition:** grouped Community sidebar; utility header/global search; breadcrumb; Resources title and one primary Share a resource action; optional featured collection/hero; discovery tabs (For you, Featured, Recent, Popular, Staff Picks); a filter/sort row; primary resource-card grid; optional contextual rail for popular topics, recently added and continue reading. Keep 24–32 px between major groups and let the card grid breathe. A compact view may exist for power users, but Comfortable is default.

**Resource card anatomy:** format icon/thumbnail; title; concise value statement; technology/category chips; source/author; difficulty and estimated time; updated/reviewed date; save control; optional trust/review status. Do not invent ratings or review states. Show a clear external-link cue for off-platform resources.

**Creative variants approved for prototype:** featured collection with editorial illustration; photo/diagram-led collection tiles; dense list mode; resource type colour accents; soft forest/teal gradient wash on one featured surface; muted technical thumbnails. Keep type/status colours semantically clear. Avoid making every card bright or illustrated.

## 12.2 Resource detail and reader — PP-RES-002 / PP-RES-003

**Primary region:** document/PDF/video/article preview. **Left or inline rail:** thumbnails/page navigation or article table of contents. **Right contextual rail:** resource details, tags, difficulty, estimated time, version, last reviewed, source, author, licence/attribution and save/share/report actions. Related resources and community discussion sit below or in a secondary tab; do not compete with reading.

For document preview, show page number, zoom, fit-to-width, download/open and accessible text alternative where available. For video, show captions/transcript when available. For articles, use Reading density, narrow line length and section anchors. External destinations must be identified before opening. The viewer must fail gracefully when preview is unsupported and offer a safe open/download alternative if permission allows.

## 12.3 Share Resource wizard — PP-RES-004 / PP-RES-005

Use a visible three-step stepper: **Details → Content → Review & publish**. Allow Back, Save draft and resume. Details fields: title, short description, resource type (Guide, Documentation, Tool, Template, Course, Video, Cheat sheet, Lab, Repository, Article), category, canonical technology tags, difficulty, intended audience, language and estimated time (if known). Content source: upload file, paste URL or write article. Review fields: author/source, attribution, licence/usage rights, version, last reviewed, visibility and guidelines acknowledgement.

Preview the resource as a recipient will see it. Show validation inline. Upload cards use `PP-UPLOAD-001` and all real lifecycle states. Scan, link validation and rights/attribution checks must be visible. A completed upload is not the same as a published resource. If a resource requires moderation, show “Submitted for review” and let the contributor track status.

## 12.4 Saved resources and collections — PP-RES-006

Tabs: All saved, Reading list, My collections, Recently viewed. Cards may use the perspective carousel pattern only for curated featured collections; the user's full saved library should remain a sortable list/grid. Collection metadata: name, description, cover/representative resource, count, visibility, last updated. Resource status: Saved, Not started, In progress, Completed where measurable. Resume reading at last position only when the source and permissions support it. Unsave never deletes the canonical resource.

## 12.5 Signup and email verification — PP-ID-001 to PP-ID-004

**Signup composition:** split-screen on wide desktop. One side carries PacketPulse's promise, a restrained network/engineering motif and concise trust copy; the other side contains the account form. Avoid overly large marketing copy or moving decorative graphics. On mobile, use a single-column form. Support email/password or passkey as the core path. Google/GitHub buttons are prototype-approved; the real identity provider is enabled only when configured and tested. If mocked, label it as a prototype action and never create a false authenticated state.

Form components: email, password with visibility toggle, password guidance, terms/privacy consent, create account action, existing-account sign-in, provider options and recovery route. Email verification uses a 6-digit code with expiry, resend cooldown, attempt-limit feedback and change-email action. Focus moves to the code field; paste of the full code is supported; announce errors accessibly.

## 12.6 Personalised onboarding — PP-ID-005 to PP-ID-007

A progress stepper guides **Role → Interests → Goals**. Role cards: Student, Engineer, Specialist, Mentor, Recruiter. Interests use canonical skill taxonomy tokens and searchable multi-select chips; allow custom suggestions through taxonomy workflow rather than silently creating taxonomy entries. Goals: Learn, Practice, Contribute, Mentor, Find opportunities. Optional steps may be skipped. Show “You can change this later”. Completion configures starting recommendations, not permissions or recruiter visibility.

## 12.7 Professional Profile — PP-ID-008 to PP-ID-012

The profile identity hero includes avatar, name, professional headline, optional location/timezone, short introduction and Edit Profile action. Banner/cover artwork may use a subtle network motif or member-selected image; avoid decorative data panels that compete with identity. Show verification labels with explicit type. The profile uses tabs/sections: Overview, Skills, Experience, Education & Certifications, Contributions, Learning, Mentoring, Activity and Achievements, rendered only when relevant data exists.

Skills are grouped by evidence state: Self-declared; Assessment verified; Experience verified; Certification verified; PP verified. Each verified state opens an explanation of evidence, issuer/reviewer, criteria, date, expiry and appeal path where permitted. Community contribution reputation is shown separately from verified skills. Profile completeness guidance is optional, dismissible, useful and non-judgmental. An identity timeline is optional and member-controlled.

## 12.8 Visibility Preview — PP-ID-013

Preview selector: My view, Community member view, Recruiter after forwarding. The implementation must call the same audience/field-level visibility rules used by the actual backend; it must not construct a front-end-only approximation. Hidden fields show a brief “Only you can see this” reason where useful. Date of birth, compensation, private notes and resume links follow DEC-015 and consent. Public web preview remains off by default and must not be implied by Community preview.

## 12.9 Account Control Centre — PP-ACC-001 to PP-ACC-006

The Account Settings overview uses spacious grouped cards with clear category descriptions and direct edit/Manage actions. Categories: Personal Information; Profile Visibility; Notifications; Login & Security; Sessions; Connected Accounts; Privacy & Data Rights; Billing & Payments when relevant. Individual detail screens use clear sections and save feedback.

Security page: password/passkey and MFA status, recovery options, active sessions, sign out other sessions, security events. Connected Accounts: provider icon, connected identity, status, requested scopes and unlink action. Prevent unlinking the last viable recovery route; step-up where required. Privacy/Data Rights: consents, export request, erasure request, request status and retained-data explanation. Deletion belongs in a distinct danger zone with consequences, re-authentication and explicit confirmation.

# 14. Media, recordings and embedded-file ecosystem

This section is an Approved Prototype specification, researched against official project documentation available on 26 November 2026. It is a recommended architecture for review, not proof of package installation or production approval. Repository compatibility, exact versions, transitive dependencies, security and licence terms must be checked before adoption.

## 14.1 Recommended stack

| Layer | Preferred candidate | Why it fits | Review notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| React playback | **Video.js 10 — `@videojs/react` (10.7.0 researched 26 Nov 2026)** | Stable v10 release; React-first, TypeScript and Tailwind patterns; player UI can be composed and branded; supports direct media and provider/stream integrations | Use only required adapters; v10 is a rewrite, not a drop-in v8 upgrade. The npm `video.js` package may still refer to v8; use the v10 framework package shown in official docs |
| Advanced streaming alternative | **Shaka Player** | HLS/DASH adaptive playback and specialist offline/EME capabilities | Separate alternative, not another default dependency. Evaluate only if DRM/offline/advanced stream requirements justify it |
| Upload UI | **Uppy React headless/hooks** | Modular, MIT-licensed uploader with React integration and custom UI composition | Use headless/hooks for PacketPulse styling; Dashboard is useful for prototypes or a quick baseline |
| Resumable transport | **Tus (`@uppy/tus`) and compatible server such as tusd** | Protocol-based pause/resume across interrupted networks; suitable for large recording uploads | Needs an actual client/server implementation and storage integration; verify existing backend before adding server infrastructure |
| Audio waveform visualisation | **WaveSurfer.js 8.1.0** | Interactive waveform, regions, timeline, hover and record plugins for audio-only recordings | Optional visual layer, not a replacement for Video.js; BSD-3-Clause; large/streamed assets may need pre-decoded peaks |
| PDF preview | **React-PDF + PDF.js** | React-friendly PDF rendering; MIT wrapper; Mozilla PDF.js viewer/renderer ecosystem | Test large files, worker configuration, text/annotation layers, SSR and memory on mobile |
| Office preview | **Isolated conversion to PDF** or separately licensed provider | One consistent reader UX and avoids blindly embedding office files | If considering ONLYOFFICE Community Edition, AGPLv3 obligations for SaaS/proprietary integration and branding restrictions need legal review; compare Developer/Commercial licensing |
| Media processing | **FFmpeg candidate** | Thumbnail extraction, metadata, audio/video transcode and HLS renditions | FFmpeg is LGPL by default with optional GPL parts; build flags/codecs may change obligations. Patent/licence review is required; do not assume all prebuilt binaries have the same licence |

**Current-version note:** Video.js official changelog lists v10.7.0 on 24 November 2026. Video.js 10.0.0 became generally available on 1 October 2026. Official v10 documentation lists first-class React, TypeScript and Tailwind support, and source support including HLS, DASH, YouTube, Vimeo, Mux and Cloudflare Stream. HLS/DASH playback engines are selectable adapters, so do not install every adapter by default.

## 14.2 Content/source type resolver

Build one PacketPulse `MediaResource` resolver that reads server-approved metadata and chooses the appropriate viewer. Do not rely only on a filename extension and do not pass arbitrary markup to a browser renderer.

- **Direct video/audio:** native source where suitable; Video.js 10 custom UI.
- **HLS/DASH:** Video.js with the matching adapter; test browser-native HLS path and supported MSE path.
- **External providers:** provider-specific adapter/embed from a server-validated canonical provider ID.
- **PDF:** React-PDF/PDF.js reader.
- **Images:** optimised image viewer with zoom, alt text and download permission.
- **Text/Markdown/code:** safe internal reader; syntax highlighting for code; sanitised Markdown; no arbitrary HTML.
- **CSV/tabular:** bounded, virtualised preview; do not execute formulas, macros or external references.
- **Office:** safe isolated conversion/preview or commercial editor after licensing/security decision.
- **Archive/executable/unknown:** metadata and permission-aware download only; never inline execute.

## 14.3 PacketPulse player experience

A player composition can include: branded poster/thumbnail, play/pause, seek bar, current time/duration, volume, playback rate, quality selection where the source supports it, captions/subtitles, transcript, chapters, fullscreen, Picture-in-Picture where supported, source/provider label, error/retry and resume. Use controls appropriate to the source; hide unavailable controls rather than rendering fake capability.

Recording details may combine a video stage with a chapter list, transcript/search, resource metadata, author/source, notes, related resources and discussion. Desktop can use a contextual rail; tablet may collapse it; mobile should turn it into tabs or sheets. Keep the player responsive and let users enter a focused/theater mode without losing the resource parent context.

**Accessibility:** captions/subtitles and transcripts are first-class. Show whether captions are human-reviewed, generated or unavailable. Keyboard operation, visible focus, accessible labels, audio descriptions where available, playback speed, transcript links and reduced-motion support should be part of acceptance testing. Avoid autoplay with sound. Respect user preferences and provider policies.

## 14.4 Upload experience and processing states

The upload component should have both a compact inline card and an expanded manager. Required states: idle, selected, validating, queued, uploading, paused, resuming, retrying, interrupted, complete, scanning, quarantined, processing, preview-ready, failed, cancelled and rejected. Show per-file progress, aggregate progress, size, time remaining only when meaningful, retry reason, pause/resume/cancel, remove/replace and processing steps. Do not claim completion before the server confirms it.

Use Uppy headless components/hooks to build a PacketPulse-owned UI. Use Tus where compatible for resumability. Store binary files in object storage, metadata in PostgreSQL, and expose short-lived signed URLs. Scan and validate before publication. Thumbnail/transcode jobs run in isolated workers and should be idempotent.

## 14.5 External provider and file security

- Accept only HTTPS and an approved provider list for embeds.
- Normalise URLs and resolve canonical provider IDs server-side.
- Do not store/render raw user-supplied iframe or HTML.
- Apply CSP `frame-src` only for providers actually enabled; check `postMessage` origin; use iframe sandbox and permission policies as compatible with provider requirements.
- Protect server-side URL resolvers against SSRF, private IPs, loopback, link-local addresses, unsafe redirects and DNS rebinding.
- Validate MIME/signature, file size, extension policy and ownership; scan files and quarantine suspicious ones.
- Process files in isolated workers with limits; never execute macros, scripts or archive contents in the application origin.
- Preserve attribution, licence, source and takedown status.

# 15. Parent-child, nested and contextual screen patterns

Nested UI should feel like an understandable journey through a workspace. A parent page remains the user's anchor; child screens should make it easy to understand where they came from, why this child exists and how to return.

## 15.1 Navigation composition options

| Pattern | Best for | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Parent page + child route | Full detail pages, readers, editors, complex forms, settings pages | URL is shareable; browser Back/Forward works; breadcrumb and local nav persist |
| Route-backed drawer/sheet | Inspecting a candidate, resource, member or queue item while retaining list context | URL identifies selected item; close/back restores parent filters, scroll and focus |
| Inline expansion | Small supplementary detail, lightweight explanation, row preview | No new modal stack; keyboard and screen-reader relationship is clear |
| Split workspace | Complex review queues, resource moderation, candidate assessment | Parent list and child details can coexist on wide viewports; collapses to route/sheet on mobile |
| Multi-step child workflow | Signup, share resource, verification, account security | Step state is visible; progress is recoverable; user can go back without losing valid input |

## 15.2 State and orientation model

A nested view should preserve, as appropriate: parent route, child route/record, active tab, search, filters, sort, selected rows, scroll location, draft state and keyboard focus. Use the URL for durable/shareable state and application state for transient UI. Do not put private personal data in URL query strings. Browser Back/Forward must match the visible screen. Deep links must repeat server-side permission checks.

Use breadcrumbs when they meaningfully help orientation, not merely because a page is nested. A clear back link, section heading or context label can be better on a short flow. On mobile, substantial children generally become their own full-screen route with an obvious back action; desktop drawers should not become tiny mobile dialogs for long-form content. Avoid modal-on-modal.

## 15.3 Shared nested-screen components

- `WorkspaceShell`: global sidebar, top bar and global utilities.
- `SectionNav`: module-specific navigation and tabs.
- `PageContextHeader`: parent name, breadcrumb/back, title, description and actions.
- `NestedRouteFrame`: shared child page structure and route transition.
- `ContextDrawer`: route-aware detail/preview panel.
- `MasterDetailWorkspace`: list/queue + detail pane.
- `ChildStepFlow`: resilient stepper for multi-stage actions.
- `ReturnContext`: restores parent filters, row selection, scroll and focus.
- `MobileChildHeader`: visible back path and child title.

## 16.3 Pattern component behaviours

Every motif may have several variations: sparse/dense, thin/bold, cropped/full, static/animated, monochrome/multicolour, calm/expressive and light/dark. Use patterns on card headers, card corners, hero overlays, profile covers, resource features, onboarding accents, media progress, section dividers, empty states and branded moments. A card can use an image, pattern, colour block or no decoration depending on its content. The absence of a pattern is also a valid design choice.

## 16.4 Motion ideas

Waves may gently travel; a packet may move along a route; a pulse may radiate from a node; a radar arc may sweep; a shield outline may resolve into focus; a data field may subtly drift. Motion can support brand recognition, atmosphere or communicate state. Explore ambient movement where it adds life; tune it to the context and preserve comfortable reading/task completion. Respect reduced-motion and offer a static alternative. Avoid movement that competes with text or obscures controls.

# 18. Creative design and experiment governance

The component ecosystem is an invitation to build a distinct PacketPulse interface, not to collect libraries or freeze one visual style. External patterns can be studied, combined and adapted. New internal components can be created where they improve clarity or character. A component can be replaced when a better accessible implementation appears. Track the source, licence, version and adaptations, but do not let source-library defaults overrule the PacketPulse experience.

For any meaningful design experiment record: hypothesis, target journey, component/pattern variant, accessibility/performance risks, reference asset, feedback window, evidence, and next-build decision. An item labelled Approved Prototype must be included in the review build or visibly represented with a mock/sandbox state where integration is not ready. The team can later Retain, Refine, Restrict or Remove it. Production approval remains separate.
